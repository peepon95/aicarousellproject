# Editorial reference system

Use this guide for the `editorial_reference` treatment. It is derived from the
five user-supplied TikTok photo posts by `@izabellialexandra`.

## Repeatable structure

1. Open with a real personal or lived-in photograph, not generic AI art.
2. Put one direct promise over the image in oversized mixed typography. Set the
   topic phrase in an italic serif and the continuation in a heavy sans serif.
   Keep it left aligned and allow four to seven deliberate lines.
3. Follow with one real source per slide.
4. Preserve the recognizable source card: thumbnail, title, author or channel,
   and enough surrounding interface to prove where it came from.
5. Use one photograph only for the front cover. Place every source card and the
   CTA over a second tactile photograph repeated consistently across the set.
6. Add a quiet italic credit below it, such as `source: youtube`,
   `source: substack`, or `source: github`.
7. Treat the post as one standalone carousel. Do not show a list count, Part 1,
   Part 2, a red series pill, or numbered-series language on the cover.

## Dimensions

- Use 3:4 for editorial covers and reading lists. Reference files commonly use
  1440 × 1920 or equivalent.
- Use 9:16 for cinematic source lists. Reference files use 1152 × 2048 or
  equivalent.
- Keep source cards inside roughly 78 percent of canvas width and 48 percent of
  canvas height so the background remains part of the composition.

## Typography and color

- Pair an expressive serif with a neutral sans serif.
- Let cover copy overlap the person or scene when legibility remains strong.
- Use one pale accent such as blush, cream, or soft yellow.
- Keep source slides quieter than the cover. The captured source does the work.

## Editorial standards

- Prefer useful specificity over hype.
- Preserve original article and video titles when they are the recommendation.
- Credit the actual platform or publisher.
- For YouTube videos, require public oEmbed metadata and use the real thumbnail,
  title, channel, and URL. Never use a watch-page screenshot or error player.
- Prefer YouTube's 16:9 `maxresdefault` thumbnail and fall back to `mqdefault`.
  Fit the whole thumbnail inside the card. Never crop the top, bottom, embedded
  title, face, or artwork to force a decorative aspect ratio.
- Never produce a blank YouTube slide. A single-video breakdown repeats its
  verified source card on every derived lesson slide.
- Do not add invented UI, fake engagement numbers, fake browser chrome, or
  model-generated screenshots.
- Do not use em dashes or en dashes in hooks, descriptions, captions, or CTAs.
- Do not imitate a creator’s identity, face, or exact composition. Reuse the
  system: direct cover, honest source, tactile background, restrained credit.
